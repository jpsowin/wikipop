# WikiPop social rules

Rules for every Instagram carousel. Keep them general: no rule should name a specific article.
Scripts and people both read this file, so each rule is one short line.

## Order
- Each day: the WikiPop (trending) carousel goes out first, the obscure carousel after.
- Never say "yesterday" in any copy.

## Slide text (Wikipedia-style slides)
- Use the article's own words. Cutting is fine; never show "[...]" or "…" for a cut. End the cut sentence with a period and start the next one cleanly.
- Drop technical clutter: birth and death dates in parentheses, pronunciations, foreign-script names, citation marks.
- Every slide has a heading. Slide 1: the article title. Later slides: the section name, or a short fitting heading (like "Origins") when the passage comes from the intro.
- Later slides show the article title small and grey above the heading.
- No blue link text. Nothing on an image can be tapped.
- Text must fit the card. Trim words instead of shrinking the font.

## Highlights
- One or two highlights per slide, on the most surprising, concrete phrase: the hook a scroller reads first.
- Never highlight two phrases that say the same thing. Keep the punchier one, usually a named thing.
- Don't highlight dry specifics (distances, institution names, dates, measurements) unless they are the joke.
- Keep highlights short: about 3 to 10 words, never a whole paragraph.
- Highlight famous names (well-known people, bands, brands). They catch the eye, so put them in the cover line too.

## Picking slides
- Pick the weirdest, funniest, most concrete passages. Skip jargon-heavy ones and ones that need lots of cuts.
- Every slide must make sense alone. Skip a passage whose example depends on setup the slide doesn't show.
- Three slides by default. Go up to 10 when the article has many great items (for example a list of people): one item per slide, each with its photo.
- A weak article (a short stub, no image, dry) moves to the end of the queue instead of going out.

## Images
- The image fills its box edge to edge: no borders, scan edges, or letterboxing. Zoom in until they're gone.
- Faces sit above the caption box. Cropping hair is fine.
- Any Wikipedia image is fine, including fair-use posters.
- Films use the poster, not a photo of one actor. Show the whole poster, or crop so the title or the people stay fully visible.
- No usable image: propose a few related Commons photos for review before posting.

## WikiPop (trending) carousel
- Only feature a pick when we know why it's trending. If the reason is unclear, take the next most-viewed article instead.
- Skip pages that trend from bots or spam, not readers (for example .xxx and .xyz).
- Cover kicker: "Trending on Wikipedia · N views", with "Trending" in yellow.
- Cover hook: one line that names the people who make the story land.
- Slide 2: three or four short fact bullets. Never "we're not sure why."

## Obscure carousel
- Blue dot background, black-bordered white card, and the "WikiPop! Obscure" sticker at the bottom right.

## Captions
- Start with the title and a colon, then the single most fun fact, in plain English. No hashtags, no emoji.
- Trending: "About N people looked it up on Wikipedia." Not "in a single day."
- End with the image credit: "Image: Author, License, via Wikimedia Commons."
- Tag an account only when certain (Wikidata's Instagram ID, or a link from the person's official site).
- Flag dark, political, or sexual picks for review before they post.
